Finding your First World War ancestor near Arras and Vimy

A free guide for Canadian and New Zealand families preparing a visit to the battlefields of Artois.

Before you travel

A few hours of research at home will turn your trip into a real pilgrimage: you will know where your relative served, where he is buried or remembered, and which places to visit. Everything below uses official, online databases.

1
Gather what the family knows. Full name, approximate date of birth, home town, regiment or battalion, service number if you have it. Old letters, photos and medals often hold these details.
2
Check whether he died in the war. The Commonwealth War Graves Commission database lists Commonwealth servicemen who died, with their grave or memorial.
CWGC – Find War Dead ↗
3
Find his service file in your national archives (see below). It tells you his unit, which in turn tells you where he fought.
4
Plan your route. Note the cemetery, memorial or battlefield you want to see, and check the visitor information before you go.

🇨🇦 For Canadian families

Service files

Library and Archives Canada has put almost all Canadian Expeditionary Force service files online, usually with the full file and the attestation paper.

  • Search by last name only, as some entries only show an initial.
  • Try spelling variations and the * wildcard (Fran* for Frank, Francis, François).
  • Early numbers with an “A” prefix were often changed to a “4”: try both.

Library and Archives Canada – First World War personnel records ↗

Those who did not come home

The Canadian Virtual War Memorial, run by Veterans Affairs Canada, is a registry of Canadians who died in uniform, with photos and stories added by families.

Canadian Virtual War Memorial ↗

The Canadian National Vimy Memorial bears the names of Canadian soldiers killed in France who have no known grave. If your relative is listed there, the CWGC record will say so.

🇳🇿 For New Zealand families

Start with the Online Cenotaph

Auckland War Memorial Museum's Online Cenotaph is a national database of people who served New Zealand, with records, photos and stories you can add to. It is also the easiest place to find a service number.

Online Cenotaph ↗

Service records

Archives New Zealand has digitised First World War service records, viewable online in its Collections search.

  • Search by name or, better, by service number.
  • Use * to match variants (John Sm*).
  • Tick “Military Personnel Files” and “Digital copy available” to filter.

Archives NZ – WWI service records ↗

Was he a tunneller? If his file mentions the New Zealand Tunnelling Company, a visit to the Wellington Quarry in Arras is a must: the tunnels his company dug still carry the names of New Zealand towns.

Practical tips

  • Note the plot and row from the CWGC record: cemeteries can be large.
  • Bring a small poppy, a photo or a few words to leave at the grave.
  • Allow a full day for Vimy and Arras: there is a lot to take in.
  • Check opening times and tour details before you go.
